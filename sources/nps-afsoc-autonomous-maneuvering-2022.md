# NPS thesis: Merlin and AFSOC defensive maneuvering

Added 2026-10-04. Student research, not a contract award. The PDF stays on the Naval Postgraduate School archive. It is not hosted here.

**Autonomous Maneuvering: A Defense Advantage for AFSOC Aircraft.** Randy S. Buckley and Jonathan D. Pryor. Naval Postgraduate School, June 2022. Approved for public release. [Calhoun 10945/70640](https://hdl.handle.net/10945/70640).

Advisors: Leo J. Blanken and Brian H. Greenshields. Keywords on the archive record: Merlin Labs, autonomous, optionally manned, defensive maneuver.

## What the thesis says

On page 7, the authors write that in 2020 SOCOM and AFSOC authored a concept of employment for a remotely piloted AC-130J and selected Merlin Labs to develop a solution for basic flight operations. That is their account. This note does not have the concept-of-employment document.

They describe a Merlin-equipped aircraft as add-on servos, sensors, and computers on an existing plane. The software uses GPS, ADS-B, and a radar altimeter. Models are trained on simulations and survey flights of a surrogate aircraft, then transferred toward a C-130J so most test flights stay off the expensive airplane. As of the thesis, the system still needed a remote operator. Commands they list: heading, altitude, airspeed, takeoff from a specified point, and landing on a specified runway.

The research frame is an alliance of Merlin Labs, Naval Surface Warfare Center Dahlgren, USSOCOM PEO-FW Gunships, AFSOC A5, and the NPS team, described as a cooperative research agreement through NPS. Deliverables named in the thesis are a concept of operations, an outbrief to AFSOC, and research notes. Not a purchase.

Their expected schedule, written into the thesis, was planning in the second half of 2021, concept design through July 2022, C-130J integration in August to October 2022, and a flight test window through December 2022. Those are planned dates in a June 2022 thesis, not results.

One assumption, labeled as an assumption, is that a Merlin system would be permanently installed on AC-130J aircraft. Do not cite that as something that happened.

The conclusion says the authors' work with Merlin was about replicating known defensive maneuvers from Air Force tactics publications, so a machine could hold more maneuvers than a crew can memorize. That is a recommendation for later research.

A line on page 27 gives 18 November 2022 as the date of a Merlin Pilot takeoff, course change, and landing on a civilian King Air, flown from a tablet. The thesis cover date is June 2022, so that date does not fit the document. Do not use it as a verified flight until there is another source.
