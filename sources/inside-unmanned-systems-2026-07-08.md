# Inside Unmanned Systems, 8 July 2026

Richard Thomas, [Merlin Labs: Bringing Autonomy to the Air Force’s Bread-and-Butter Fleet](https://insideunmannedsystems.com/merlin-labs-bringing-autonomy-to-the-air-forces-bread-and-butter-fleet/). Interview with Matt George at SOF Week. A trade-press interview, not a contract document. The photo is not hosted here.

## What George said

George’s argument is that Air Force autonomy matters more on the aircraft already flying than on a new exquisite platform. The article quotes him: “Autonomy is going to be the future of air power,” and “What we’re really proud to do at Merlin is to deliver autonomy into that bread and butter of the Air Force.”

He said the stack is meant to fly the airplane (control, procedures, airspace) so the pilot can act as a mission commander, including directing other aircraft. “That’s what we’re doing for SOCOM and AFSOC.” He compared the approach to an F-150: lots of repetitions on a common fleet before the same system moves to a rarer aircraft.

On speed, he said Merlin cannot move at the pace of the defense primes and has to move at the pace of adversaries. He grouped Merlin with what he called neo-primes, companies he said are collectively investing hundreds of millions in Air Force autonomy. That is his framing, not a disclosed Merlin spend.

## Claims to separate from the interview

The article says the Air Force operates more than 400 C-130Js and that roughly 300 KC-135s remain active. Those are the magazine’s fleet counts.

It says the C-130J work traces to 2019 precursor flights on a Twin Otter. It says a February 2026 shareholder letter from George called Merlin the sole prime on SOCOM’s C-130J autonomy program, described that program as production-focused, with milestones and a path to fleet-wide deployment. That is the letter as reported by the magazine. This note does not have the letter, and “path to fleet-wide” is not a funded fleet buy.

The article says Merlin completed a preliminary design review and was working with the Air Force and AFSOC toward fielding. It does not mention the critical design review Merlin announced on 4 June 2026. The July article is behind that company release.

It also says Merlin moved from SBIR contracts, through other-transaction agreements, to a nine-figure production contract, with AFSOC first and later relationships with Air Mobility Command and Air Combat Command. “Nine-figure” matches the publicly described C-130J IDIQ ceiling. A ceiling is not money obligated, and this article does not give a funded amount.

The photo is a Kentucky Air National Guard C-130J leaving Karup, Denmark, on 8 June 2026, credited to the Air National Guard (Lt. Col. Dale Greer) and Merlin Labs. The caption does not say Merlin equipment was on that airplane.
